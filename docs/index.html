Search-Performance Signals for Content Review Prioritization

A public-safe ranking study that uses search-performance signals to prioritize content pages for human review and identify measurable CTR opportunity.

Praveena Danthapally · FlyRank ML Internship Capstone · 2026

Abstract

This study asks whether search-performance signals can be used to rank content pages for human review and identify pages with measurable CTR opportunity. I built a Random Forest regression workflow using content, visibility, engagement, and trend signals available at the content-page observation level. The Week-5 evaluation measured an NDCG of 0.4629, compared with 0.4391 for the Week-4 baseline, while a stricter client-grouped validation using unseen clients produced an NDCG of 0.8157. The resulting ranking was converted into a five-row action queue containing priority scores and human-review reason codes. The findings support directional decision-support for content prioritization, but they do not establish causality, guarantee future performance, or justify automatic content changes.

1. Introduction / Problem Statement

Content teams can have many pages competing for limited optimization and editorial attention. A practical challenge is therefore not only identifying pages that could matter, but deciding which pages should be investigated first.

This capstone addresses that prioritization problem using the Refresh / Content Opportunity Scoring direction. The goal is to transform observable search-performance signals into a ranked review queue.

Research question:
Can search-performance signals be used to rank content pages for human review and identify pages with measurable CTR opportunity?

The decision supported by this analysis is the prioritization of pages for human investigation. The model is deliberately treated as decision-support rather than an automated content optimization system.

In particular, a high model score does not mean that a page should automatically be rewritten, redirected, deleted, or otherwise changed. Human review is required before any action.

3. Methodology

Method choice

I used a Random Forest Regressor to estimate the next observed CTR from information available in the modeling data.

Random Forest was selected because it can represent nonlinear relationships between search visibility, content characteristics, engagement, and trend signals without requiring a linear functional relationship.

Features

The model used 27 observable features covering search demand, competition, content characteristics, historical visibility, engagement, recency, traffic, and trend signals.

Examples include search volume, competition, CPC, word count, character count, impressions, clicks, sessions, users, engagement rate, scroll rate, AI traffic percentage, content age, days since update, and trend percentage.

Target definition

The target is future_ctr, representing the future CTR target constructed in the Week-5 modeling workflow. It is treated as a limited future-performance proxy rather than a direct measure of refresh success.

Leakage protection

Direct CTR target leakage was explicitly excluded. The current ctr and target future_ctr were not included in the predictive feature set.

The target was also checked to ensure that it was not present in the feature matrix.

Baseline

The model was compared with the transparent Week-4 baseline using NDCG, keeping the evaluation metric consistent with the ranking task.

Client-grouped validation

The stricter Week-6 validation used GroupShuffleSplit with client identifiers as groups. Training and testing clients were completely separated.

Validation property Result
Training rows 12,493
Test rows 5,424

NDCG was calculated within each unseen test client and averaged across eligible clients. This makes the Week-6 result a stricter check of cross-client generalization than an ordinary random row split.

5. Limitations & Honest Framing

This study should be interpreted as a ranking and decision-support exercise rather than a causal experiment.

  • The target is a future CTR proxy, not a direct refresh-success label.
  • The model does not prove that a content refresh causes CTR improvement.
  • A high prediction score does not guarantee future performance.
  • The Week-5 improvement over the baseline is directional and specific to its evaluation setup.
  • The Week-6 grouped validation uses unseen clients but only one grouped split, so repeated or cross-validated evaluation would provide stronger evidence.
  • Low-volume observations can have unstable CTR measurements because relatively few clicks can produce large rate changes.
  • Observational feature importance represents association and should not be interpreted as causal influence.
What the model can support: prioritizing pages for investigation.

What the model cannot establish: which content change will definitely work, whether a refresh caused an improvement, or whether a high score guarantees future traffic.

For these reasons, automatic publishing, rewriting, deletion, redirection, canonical changes, robots/indexing changes, and other important content or technical changes remain outside the system.

Human-review checklist

Before any content action, the reviewer should consider:

  1. Search intent
  2. Content quality
  3. Business relevance
  4. Search-performance context
  5. Competition
  6. Technical factors
  7. Recent changes
  8. Final action decision
No automatic content actions. The queue is a prioritization tool for human review.
